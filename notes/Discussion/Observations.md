## Co-occurrence

The high-strength connections are sometimes useful for identifying sets of connected concepts that orbit a certain term. Sometimes these suggest a school or system of thought.

- The SEP search results for "faith" are all isolated from each other, except for one grouping of concepts that clearly come from Kierkegaard ("knight," "princess," "resignation").
- The co-occurrence network for "benevolence" reveals some interesting similarities with "love" that are not picked up by the similarity network. The two terms have a direct connection that, relative to the rest of the network, is not very strong. But they are both connected to a more-densely connected bundle of nodes ("humanity," "genuine," "universal," "motivate," "delight", "pity," etc.) suggesting that they bear a common relation to another set of related concepts.

## Similar meanings?

The full semantic space of the SEP shows some similar patterns to that of the Mengzi, though how significant these are is up for debate. "Justice" and "propriety" sit right next to each other in the SEP, as do their analogues, 義 and 禮, in the Mengzi. Each one also sits in their pair's similarity networks. This is somewhat interesting, since justice is a far "thicker" term, occurring frequently in the SEP, while "propriety" is much rarer and thinner, with 59.39% of its sampled occurrences appearing in articles on Chinese philosophy.
