In my project proposal, I identified eleven terms of interest that I wanted this project to analyze. I also wrote that "adding additional terms should not significantly increase the workload." This was based on the fact that, at the time, I had automated most of the NLP pipeline, including the fetching and parsing of SEP articles. Adding another term to analysis increased the number of SEP articles processed, and therefore the time the pipeline took to run. But all this required was updating the terms list in my config file (`scripts/config.py`).

But adding more terms introduced several difficulties that I hadn't forseen. The [[Corpus Asymmetry|asymmetry in each corpus's scope]] meant that the size of the Chinese text remained the same (always the entire *Mengzi*), while the size of the English corpus increased for each new term added. Because the vocabulary of the SEP is quite diverse (varying from article to article) a larger corpus meant a more populated and less readable scatter plot. I could have set an absolute cap on the number of scatterplot nodes that get sent to the user, or decrease the number of articles returned per-term, or change how the target terms are displayed so that their labels don't crowd each other out. With enough time and enough tuning, I think I could make the larger corpus readable. But after already tuning them to produce meaningful results on the Five Constant Virtues, and losing these results in the initial pass on the full terms list, I decided to settle on the virtues for my MVP.

In the end \[I actually reduced the number of core terms for the MVP, to the "Five Constant Virtues."]
